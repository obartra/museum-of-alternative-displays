# Design review, 21 November 2026

A review of the interface on a phone and on a desktop, by day and by night, after the archive
pictures, the facts, the views and the five translations went in. It was done by the same hand
that built the pages, from screenshots, so it is a self-review and not an outside opinion.

## What was looked at

Phone at 390 by 844 and 320 by 640, desktop at 1280 by 800 and 1440 by 900, both light settings:

- the entrance, a hall, a piece page from top to bottom, the index and the about page
- the archive row with one picture and with four, and the viewer on a photograph, a tall
  drawing, a patent drawing and a film
- the views row and the line under it, in English, German, French and Chinese
- the language menu, the keyboard shortcuts panel and the bar that steps between pieces

Every page was also run through axe-core in both light settings at three widths, and the five
translations were checked for anything that runs off a 320 pixel screen.

## What holds up

- The archive row reads as mounted prints: a mat, a hairline frame, a catalogue line in the
  label typeface, the caption, then the credit in small type. On a phone it scrolls sideways
  with the next mount showing, and on a desktop it is a grid beside the model.
- The viewer puts the picture first and its record under it on a phone, and beside it on a
  desktop. Close, the browser's Back, Escape and a tap on the bare ground all shut it.
- Patent drawings sit comfortably among photographs. By night their paper is dimmed.
- The facts, set off by two rules under "What to look for", read as an aside and not as part
  of the label.

## What was changed because of the review

1. **The bar at the foot of a phone screen** let the text behind it show through as a smear.
   It is now solid.
2. **Picture credits** on the about page were 54 loose lines. They are now one block for each
   piece, with its pictures listed under its name.
3. **The entrance** offered no plain way in for someone who does not read the board as a list
   of links. Two buttons now follow the opening lines: start in hall 1, or look through the
   index.
4. **The row of places to look from** showed dead buttons until the model ran. Every button in
   it now starts the model and goes where it says.
5. **What a view shows** used to lie over the picture and vanish at the first touch. It is now
   a line under the buttons that stays. Only the note on how to move goes over the picture.
6. **Long German names** pushed the index wider than a small phone. They now wrap.

## Left as it is, with the reason

- A single archive picture takes the whole width of its column. On a desktop that gives a
  small drawing a wide mat. It was kept, because the alternative is a lonely narrow card.
- The views row scrolls sideways on a phone when a piece has three places. The cut-off button
  at the edge is the only hint. A second row of buttons would push the picture controls down.
- Authors are shown as the archive gives them, so one credit reads "Unknown".

## Not covered

- No one other than the builder has reviewed it.
- It was looked at in Chrome only, and in still screenshots. Motion, touch feel and Safari on
  an iPhone were not judged.
