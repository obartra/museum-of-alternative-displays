# Model review: designer and historian pass

Every model was rendered on its plinth with the hall lights on and off and looked at as a picture, twice over:

- **Historian (A, accuracy).** Does it look like the thing? Right parts, right arrangement, right behaviour, judged against the sources cited for the piece and what is generally known about the object.
- **Designer (D, fit).** Does it read as a made object on a phone screen, and does it sit in the museum's look: an industrial hall, steel, enamel, glass, wood?

Scores run from 1 to 5. "Before" is the first build. "After" is this pass.

Two limits, stated plainly. The review was done by the same hand that built the models, in two roles, and not by two independent people. And accuracy was judged against text sources and general knowledge of the objects: no reference photographs were opened in this pass, so proportions are plausible and not measured.

## What was wrong everywhere

| Finding | Change |
| --- | --- |
| Glass did not read as glass. Clear envelopes vanished against the whitewash. | New glass: almost nothing face on, a dark line where the wall turns away, lamps mirrored on top, and light from inside caught in the wall. Sheets of glass get ground green edges. |
| Every edge was a razor edge, which is what makes a model look low-poly. | Blocks and turned parts have eased edges by default. |
| Curves showed their facets. | More segments everywhere; turning profiles are curves through their points. |
| Surfaces were perfectly even. | Paint, brushed metal and wood grain, worked out from where a point sits on the part. |
| Lamps were coloured balls. | A bright core and a soft halo for every small light; bloom round single lamps. |
| Several pieces showed a diagram of the effect where a visitor expects the effect. | Things seen through other things are now drawn that way: a mirror image only inside its pane, a scene only through its lens, symbols only inside the combiner. Seven pieces have a button that puts your eye where the piece expects one. |
| Stills caught models before they had settled. | Boards, tiles and pins start in their settled state. |
| The plinth texts described the old models. | Rewritten where the model changed. |

## Still open

- Enlarged cutaways (field emission, micromirror, gyricon, electrowetting, interferometric modulator, the exploded LCD) are diagrams by nature. They are tidier now, not more object-like.
- The terminal, the headset and the tablet are generic. No maker's marks are reproduced.
- A pass against photographs, piece by piece, would be worth doing before anyone relies on a proportion.
